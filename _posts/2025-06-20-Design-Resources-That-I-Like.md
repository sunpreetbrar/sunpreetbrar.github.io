----

A great place to start, covers all the fundamentals. They might seem trivial, but just mastering these is way harder than it seems and is very rewarding. 
<https://youtu.be/8pmij4T2fRw?si=WIMyxfhdlaD4L9iO>

Apple's playlist teaching foundational design principles.
<https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLjODKV8YBFHbUrwP1yYtrAggFm5qN0NeG>

It is also helpful to study the greats like Dieter Rams and Paul Rand. 

I have put these resources at the top because they teach design thinking. Which helps you spot why great design works and how to take inspiration from them. Moreover all this is tools agnostic. 

Design books can be helpful. I haven't read much, although I plan to. I have read "How design makes you think" which I liked.

-------

A great reddit thread linking to resources such as textures, fonts, stock images etc. It is useful to have a quality collection of textures, well arranged in folders etc. It helps making your workflow faster and designs better. But don't go into the rabbit hole of collecting things for the sake of it, if you aren't making stuff then its useless.
<https://www.reddit.com/r/graphic_design/comments/veb3i8/free_design_resources/>

One of the best interface designer as well as brand identity work. Great for design inspiration.
<https://x.com/ayushsoni_io>

Curated designs.
<https://searchsystem.co/>
<https://themesystem.tumblr.com/>

Moreover for inspiration, especially for graphic design, print design, album covers, package design or branding I have found Pinterest and Instagram very helpful. You just need to fine tune your algorithm to serve good design work instead of brain rot (have a separate account for that).

Film posters, album covers and some concert promotional material is a great source for ideas. (I personally like Psychedelic band's posters)

------

Harish is chief designer behind CRED. Possibly India's best designed product from a startup.
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nWuk46Ifl9E&t=106s>
<https://cred.club/design>

Well, when it comes to Indian design scene, I would like to mention how game changing was early Housing.com 's design. A lot of amazing designers from that era are, ex-housing. Rahul Yadav was a visionary.

Haven't linked any tutorials to learn tools because I have not really followed any of them. I do not have the attention span to sit through them. Although they are useful to troubleshoot while working. You can learn a lot on the go.
